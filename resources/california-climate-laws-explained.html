Understand California's Climate Laws

SB 253, SB 261, and SB 219 are reshaping how companies disclose climate and sustainability data. Here's what these laws mean and how ecoPRISM helps you stay ahead.

Explore Now

What are SB 253, SB 261 & SB 219?

California has passed three major climate laws to increase transparency and accountability in corporate environmental impact

SB 253

Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act

Requires companies with $1B+ annual revenue doing business in California to publicly report global GHG emissions (Scopes 1 & 2 from 2026, Scope 3 from 2027)

Emissions Reporting

SB 261

Climate-Related Financial Risk Disclosure Act

Applies to companies with $500M+ annual revenue, requiring biennial climate risk reports aligned with the TCFD framework starting in 2026.

Risk Assessment

SB 219

Amendments to SB 253 & SB 261

Effective September 27, 2024, SB 219 updates both acts. It delays CARB's rulemaking to July 1, 2025; introduces reporting flexibility (like consolidated parent‑level filing); removes immediate filing fee requirements; and allows CARB discretion on Scope 3 timing rather than enforcing a 180‑day deadline.

Regulatory Update (as of July 2025)

A California Air Resources Board (CARB) public workshop was held on May 29, 2025, where CARB acknowledged rulemaking is behind schedule. They now aim to release draft regulations by the end of 2025, with final rules expected in late 2026.

Top Requirements & Risks

Key compliance requirements and potential risks for companies subject to California climate laws

SB 253

  • Scopes 1-2 reporting begins in 2026; Scope 3 from 2027 based on CARB schedule.
  • Assurance: Limited assurance for Scopes 1 & 2 starts 2026; Reasonable assurance for scope 1 and 2, and limited for Scope 3 by 2030
  • Penalties: Up to $500K per year for non-compliance. For the 2026 cycle CARB will exercise discretion toward entities making good-faith efforts, particularly for Scope 1 and 2 reporting.
Penalty: Up to $500K

SB 261

  • Reports must follow TCFD or ISSB frameworks: governance, strategy, risk management, metrics & targets.
  • Must be published publicly and confirmed to the California Secretary of State.
  • Penalties: Up to $50K per year for missing or insufficient reports, though previous compliance actions and good faith are considered
Penalty: Up to $50K

Compliance Tips

Best practices and strategies for successful compliance with California climate laws

  • Start establishing ESG data governance now - designate roles, build a cross-functional data team, and centralize emissions & risk data repositories
  • Prepare for third-party assurance by 2026 for Scopes 1 & 2.
  • Plan early for Scope 3: supplier engagement, data collection, and tracing upstream/downstream emissions.
  • For SB 261 compliance: map climate risks, strategies, and metrics using TCFD; align with existing reports (e.g., CDP, SASB, ISSB).
  • Monitor CARB’s final regulations by end of year 2025 – prepare to implement official guidance promptly
  • Track litigation around SB 253 and SB 261 - legal challenges could affect enforcement timing or scope

  • Entities subject to SB 253 (those with >US$1 billion in annual revenue doing business in California) are required to pay an annual fee of US$3,106.
  • Entities subject to SB 261 (those with >US$500 million in annual revenue doing business in California) are required to pay an annual fee of US$1,403.
  • Reporting entities with more than $1B in revenue are subject to both fee.
  • Annual fees are to be adjusted for inflation and fund deficit/surplus in future year.
  • Approximately 2,596 entities are estimated to be subject to SB 253.
  • Approximately 4,160 entities are estimated to be subject to SB 261.

Note: The number of entities listed above is based on staff analysis of CA Secretary of State and Dunn & Bradstreet data. CARB will be initiating a process to validate the preliminary list of covered entities.

  • SB 253 – Emissions Reporting: Entities must submit their emissions reports using CARB’s templates (to be released for public feedback by September 2025) through CARB’s system and pay the required annual fee.
  • SB 261 – Climate-Related Financial Risk Reports: Entities must publish their climate-related financial risk reports on their website, post the report link to CARB’s public docket (opening December 1, 2025), and pay the required filing fee

Companies may discuss the resilience of their strategy considering future climate change impacts under various climate scenarios. CARB encourages including qualitative scenario-based assessments where feasible and relevant, describing how the organization’s strategy may respond to potential climate-related risks and opportunities. This discussion can focus on strategic adjustments, risk mitigation measures, and long-term planning to ensure the organization remains resilient under changing climate conditions.
